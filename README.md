# Analyzing_Happiness
Analyzing Global Happiness Trends and Evaluating the Metrics of Well-being Using Python<br /><br />

Project Overview<br />
This project explores the World Happiness Report 2024, analyzing global happiness trends and critically evaluating the relevance of the metrics used to assess well-being. <br />
The aim is to determine whether the current indicators accurately reflect happiness and to propose potential enhancements or alternative measures for a more comprehensive assessment. <br />
<br />
Data Source<br />
•	Dataset: World Happiness Report 2024<br />
•	Source: Official World Happiness Report<br />
<br />

Objective<br />
•	Analyze the happiness scores of different countries.<br />
•	Evaluate the key factors contributing to happiness (e.g. GDP per capita, social support, life expectancy, freedom, generosity and corruption perceptions).<br />
•	Assess whether the chosen metrics are appropriate for measuring happiness.<br />
•	Suggest possible improvements or alternative indicators for a more holistic assessment.<br />

Methodology<br />
1.	Data Cleaning & Preparation<br />
•	Renamed and structured columns for clarity.<br />
•	Handled missing values, if any.<br />
•	Converted necessary variables into appropriate data types.<br />

2.	Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)<br />
•	Examined distributions and trends of happiness scores across regions.<br />
•	Created visualizations to compare happiness levels across different continents.<br />
•	Correlation analysis to identify relationships between happiness score and key parameters.<br />

3.	Assessment of Parameters<br />
•	Investigated whether the existing parameters adequately explain variations in happiness.<br />
•	Suggested additional factors that could enhance happiness measurement.<br />

4.	Findings & Recommendations<br />
•	Discussed key insights derived from the analysis.<br />
•	Highlighted additional factors that contribute to happiness beyond the current metrics.<br />

5. Technologies Used<br />
•	Python: Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn<br />
•	upyter Notebook for in-depth analysis and visualization<br />
•	Tableau for creating interactive visualizations, enhancing the storytelling experience with dynamic insights<br />

6. Key Takeaways<br />
•	Happiness rankings are heavily influenced by GDP per capita and social support.<br />
•	Some high-income countries rank lower than expected, indicating that non-economic factors like mental health and work-life balance may be underrepresented.<br />
•	While the current parameters offer a solid framework, incorporating cultural and psychological well-being measures could significantly refine the accuracy of the rankings.<br />
•	Additionally, countries such as India and Brazil - despite their lower happiness index scores - often display a happier population when considering factors like strong family bonds, community support, cultural resilience and a more holistic approach to life satisfaction, which are not fully captured by traditional economic metrics.
